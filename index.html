Saturday Club is a small, vetted gathering somewhere in Karachi every Saturday. To get in, you hand over your national ID. This page reads the sign-up form as a source: what it asks, who sees it, how long it lives, and who it quietly leaves at the door.

Aiman Afzal Arain · Portfolio Assignment 1

No. 00The biography.Saturday Club

A background check on the data

Heather Krause argues that a dataset needs a background check before you trust it, the same way a journalist checks a human source. She asks four questions. Here they are, turned on the club's own records.

Where

A Supabase database behind socialisewithmarzya.com. One row per person, one per booking, with photos in private storage.

Who

The host collects it, through a platform I designed and built. The collector is also the judge, and I am inside this data, not outside it.

How

Three kinds of source share one row: what the applicant types, what her documents and camera show, and what the host decides by hand.

Why

Krause calls this the most important question. The stated reason is safety: every stranger checked. But the same record also runs payments, strikes and bans long after the safety check is done.

Krause's own example showed apparent trends that were really changes in how data was collected. The map below carries the same risk: it records where the host chose venues, not where Karachi wanted to gather.

No. 02WHO SEES WHAT.Saturday Club

Power sits in one column

D'Ignazio and Klein ask us to examine power: who collects, who decides, who is collected. Reading down the columns, the host sees every field in full. The applicant cannot view her own uploaded photos, cannot read her own feedback after sending it, and never learns the host keeps private notes on her. The door is the host's own account, not separate staff.

No. 04WHERE THE CLUB HAPPENS.Saturday Club

A map that refuses to pin

The club's own rule is that nobody sees the exact location until the host offers them a seat. A map with pins would break that promise, so this one shows Karachi as hexagons by neighbourhood and shades only how many events each area has held.

Shaded areas have hosted at least one event. Exact counts per area are coming from the club's database.

What it shows. Every event so far sits in one band of central and south Karachi: Clifton, DHA Phases 5 to 7, PECHS, Sindhi Muslim, Tipu Sultan, Bahadurabad and Maskan. The north, the west and the industrial east are empty.

What the hexes hide. Equal tiles make Orangi and Clifton look the same size. They are not, in land, people, or money. The tiling is a choice that trades geography for legibility, which is Drucker's point: every chart is capta, something taken and shaped, not something given.

What the shading cannot say. An empty hex might mean nobody applied from there, or that nobody there felt the club was for them. The map shows where the club went, not where it was wanted.

No. 05A LIGHTER FORM.Saturday Club

Same safety, less data

The host's goal is real: a room where everyone has been checked. The question is whether every field earns its place. These are proposed changes to discuss with her.

Now
  • The CNIC is the only ID accepted. The age check was removed in October, but since CNICs are issued at 18 the card still sets one. Anyone without a card, or with a blocked one, cannot apply.
  • Students must upload a student ID. Working people only type a job title. Proof falls on the youngest applicants.
  • An applicant nobody ever decides on keeps her ID photos, selfie and CNIC in storage with no expiry, though the footer promises deletion.
  • Rejections may come with no reason, and the host's private notes are invisible to the person they describe.
Proposed
  • Keep the CNIC hash for one-person-one-account, and accept a passport as an alternative.
  • One proof rule for everyone, or a "vouched by a member" route instead of an ID.
  • Auto-close undecided applications 30 days after the event passes, and purge their files with the rest.
  • A short reason with every rejection, and a plain data notice on the form: what is kept, for how long, who sees it.

No. 06METHOD.Saturday Club

How this was made

I built Saturday Club (live at socialisewithmarzya.com) with Claude Code on Next.js and Supabase, hosted on Vercel. For this piece I had Claude Code audit the live code (main branch, 31 October 2026): every table and column, the row-level security rules and column grants that decide who reads what, and the scheduled jobs that delete files. That audit became the field cards, the matrix and the timeline. It also caught the plan drifting from the build: the age limit, WhatsApp automation and social-account verification were all dropped along the way. Nothing here comes from real guests. Sample values are invented, and the map holds only areas. The page itself was vibecoded and deployed the same way I taught in Session 5.

Context on the CNIC comes from Pakistani reporting. Since August 2014, no SIM card can be sold or activated without a biometric check against the CNIC. In March 2026 a Senate committee approved a bill giving NADRA explicit power to block cards, which would also cut off SIMs, banking and travel. A club that gates entry on the CNIC inherits that whole system of sorting.
